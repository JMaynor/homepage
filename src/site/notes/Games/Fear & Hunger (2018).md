# Summary

Fear & Hunger is a dungeon crawler released in 2018. It was developed by Miro Haverinen and published by Happy Paintings. In it, you play as various lost souls who've found themselves drawn to an ancient fortress and the darkness within.

Here we go. Long asked-for, long-dreaded. We're doing a silly SFW mod found on Nexus to avoid the panopticon's cruel and miserly gaze. (I know, it's gross and dumb and stupid. Literally no one likes or wants it.) https://www.nexusmods.com/fearandhunger/mods/13 Anyway, certain miscreants wanted me to play this. I have heard that there are interesting things to be gleaned and that it is not just suffering and dread, so we shall see.

# Review

I'm super conflicted on this one! The pee-pee poo-poo bobs and vagene aspect of the game is really in your face. I don't think I've ever played an edgier game quite honestly, even with the silly censorship mod in play. And so I ask myself: "Does it *need* to be there?" I've really gone back and forth on this. Fear & Hunger is a grimdark world. I totally get that and understand that it's a core part of the world's design. What I wonder is, would enough of the grimdark atmosphere survive if we took out some of the more reprehensible bits? My inclination is to say yes it would. First and foremost because *even with the mod* I got perfectly well that aesthetic. It's coming across loud and clear.

The Fear & Hunger fan tulpa that I'm fighting against right now, their claim is that if you want something less squicky, there's more than enough out there. Why do we need to unsquickify this thing that's sitting over there, hurting no one? My problem is....not all lines we're crossing in this game feel like they're being crossed in good taste. Do we really need an anal bleeding status effect? I just think that we could be more artful about this if we pulled a couple of our punches. I think things would go down much smoother. Video games on the whole are in friendship conquers all territory to the point that they're frequently embarrassing. The money men rule these streets. I feel like you don't really have to go "all the way" to get across to players that you are being different and things are "real" here.

I would just like to go up to the dev and ask them what they might create if they pulled back just a bit. I am not asking for kiddy time play hour, I promise you. But having never heard an interview from the dev, if indeed any exist, my feeling is that Fear & Hunger gives me that sense of "I have an idea and I need to get it all down now because I might never get the chance again."  You see it most often with first-time devs or directors. They've got an idea and they feel like they have to fully capture it otherwise they may not get another chance to fully explore it. So we have Fear & Hunger which veers, sometimes to the point of absurdity, into edgy edgy dark territory.

I think were I younger, I would fall more strongly on the side of "let art be art". Let it push boundaries and explore dark themes. Being older and wiser, I understand that we're playing with live ammo here. And if you're going to be playing with live ammo, you have a responsibility to handle it with care. I do not think Fear & Hunger is handling it with care. I think there are multiple instances of it just being dark for dark's sake. Or frankly for a laugh. And that if you pushed the creator on "why is this here" they would fall back on it being a crapsack world and the thing in question is yet another example of the crapsackiness. I wanna be exceptionally clear. I am not saying that you cannot depict dark stuff. Depiction is not endorsement. Anyone who is not a dipshit gets that. But there has to be a universal agreement that there is a difference between making something for yourself and publishing it for the world. The moment you put out something for the rest of the world to see, you do have to be able to justify it. Some parts of this game give the sense of an adolescent who has recently been given new tools. And who is too young to have a respect for when and how to make use of them. Very edgy teen talking on forums with other edgy teens trying to one-up each other with dead baby jokes.

I have an example that I think demonstrates this well. Bloodborne. Bloodborne plays with a number of similar themes to Fear & Hunger. In particular, it trucks in the loss of bodily autonomy, specifically in a sexual sense. We see multiple women who have already, or are in the process of being impregnated against their will by eldritch beings. We focus more on the body horror of that scenario than the psychology of bearing that abuse in Bloodborne. But we're in the same lane. And my point is that *no one* is saying Bloodborne is just being edgy for edge's sake, because Fromsoft is handling that live ammo with a lot more care. Firstly, we're not directly depicting anything, but rather we're more wisely alluding to it. It's also not just played for laughs. My understanding is that Fear & Hunger 2 lightens up *to some degree*, so my guess would be that I am vindicated in this matter and that we reined it in a little bit.

Moving on to gameplay. Too hard, didn't like. Fucking with my saving in particular is a no-go for me and I will move heaven and earth to undo what's been done. As evidenced by the fact that I employed not one, but two mods that revolved around making it where I could save anywhere. It's just something I don't have the time for. Fuck you, do it again. Fuck you, do it again. I'm sure it would have created a rather large dopamine hit repeatedly struggling to get through some section and then finally getting a good run where everything worked out, I open up a shortcut, and then make it to a save point. Don't care. Don't have the time for it. I turned that off and moved on with my life.

I find it hard to respect the game design when all it amounts to is a series of knowledge checks. It just feels cheap to me. The games that I most respect are those that have some level of complexity to them. That allow for emergent design where the player can stumble upon or purposefully discover solutions with creative thinking. That can't happen in this game. If you swear off the wiki, the game functions as a "fuck you, do it again" scenario generator. I just don't find that fun. Conceptually it's enjoyable, sure, to have a game where knowledge of its systems allows you to break it over your knee. But there being no real skill aspect in play just makes the whole thing feel empty. The goal, in essence, is to use your game knowledge to avoid as many of the game's systems as possible. Avoid enemies until you get weapons and party members. When fighting them, follow this script to avoid certain attacks or minimize the likelihood of those attacks happening. If you memorize the digits of pi, you certainly can then recite the digits of pi if asked. This is a true thing.

Does a game *need* to be fun? No. My ur-example of this is Papers, Please. Papers, Please is not fun to play; It's fairly miserable in fact. But it's very cannily designed in a way where its systems all interconnect and they reveal truths. You find yourself naturally put into a position where you dehumanize real people because their real lived circumstances have inconvenienced you. You want to process as many people as quickly as possible because your family is on the line and the more border crossings you get through, the more you are paid. Every inevitable mistake feels like a knife between the ribs. It's so great and none of it feels good. I give this example to show that I understand unfun design can serve a higher artistic purpose that is worthy. The unrelenting difficulty in Fear & Hunger certainly characterizes this world. I guess all I can say is that I am getting less juice from this squeeze than I am from a Papers, Please.

I've been pretty hard on Fear & Hunger, but at the beginning of the review, I said I was conflicted. So let's move on to the aspects of it that I like. I'm pretty hot on the lore. A surprise given I've [[Blog/2026-03-26 Let the lore die. Kill it, if you have to.\|turned against lore so strongly]] in the past few years. But I do really find it compelling the foul influence of these old gods and the attempts of humanity to install themselves as new gods in a dim world. I think the decision to take the real world and file off the serial numbers works well here. I would have been less interested in a ground-up fantasy world that started showing me its nerd maps and shit. The little snippets that we get of the wider world intrigue in much the same way that the older Souls games did. This is what showing restraint can give you. The realization that this is just Europe. That the dark continent, the old old world is North America is delightful.

I'll also add that the Engrish lens we get all of this through is actually kind of additive. It gives this enjoyable feeling that we're getting a word of mouth read of the actual event that's gone through several layers of the telephone game (and more than one language) before it got to us. At first I found it a bit amateurish, and to some degree I still think that. (The misspellings specifically, I can fuck with the odd word choice and occasional bad grammar.) But on the whole, it puts you the player on uneven-footing and creates an interesting sense of discovery. Reminds me a bit back in the day when I was on teh forumz getting news of the Kingdom Hearts final mix releases that were exclusive to Japan. Any info I got of that content was coming through layers of fan translations. Had this great desire to know more. That layer of fiction served to fire my imagination. And to a lesser extent that is happening here. Deliberate? I've no idea, but the effect is there all the same.

I like the layout of the dungeons of Fear & Hunger. To name-drop Dark Souls yet again, the shortcuts are well-placed. I enjoy that everything is layered on top of each other and by the end of the game I could see multiple paths to get around. It does the satisfying circling back to Firelink approach that has had me yearning for FromSoft to get their shit back in order for the last 10 years. And despairing knowing that they never will. I think in this case that central hub of the world being the prisons? Or I guess the entrance as well sort of. Nothing is actually that far away from anything else, once you have all the doors open. Another aspect of the layout that I like is the partial randomization. The floors themselves and the general layout staying the same, with the individual rooms and walls getting shuffled adds a nice little layer of cohesion between the story and the gameplay. (The premise that this is a dark place where the influence of the old gods is physically warping the geometry.) No idea if there's any pattern to that, or maybe just some predetermined set of possible layouts. I just noticed that maps of the world on the wiki did not match my experience. And having seen bits of another playthrough, that it too differed. An interesting design decision that I approve of.

Some of the sprite designs are quite inspired. Love some of these weird little freaks. Multiple times I said out loud while playing "Oh, I love him". The god of the depths, the crow mauler, the body snatcher, the scarabs. I like when it's not just dicks, dicks, dicks.

I have one last instance of goodwill I'd like to note. Which is the willingness to be inexplicable. Maybe this is an odd thing to praise? Did I not just lambaste the game earlier for it's approach of being a series of knowledge checks? Well, I think one has to separate things that need be known in order to progress, vs extraneous little details that are merely hidden away. These are your Ash Lakes vs your level Adaptability or die. I respect the confidence of a developer that is willing to hide away party members (if you talk to Moonless, you can recruit them with meat). And many other such "Did you know that if you..." conversations one might have with another player. Those are good and make games deeper. They create a culture of collaboration between players, as long as those hidden details aren't required for winning.

I'm generally glad I played the game and have personal experience with it now. Fear & Hunger was not so hostile that I am not semi-intrigued by the idea of the sequel. I like Majora's Mask, far and away my fave Zelda. And the idea of it lightening its shit up seems lovely. It won't be the next thing I play, but it's in the queue somewhere. I guess. I suppose. Perhaps.
