# Review

A triumph in character design. Look Outside is fun, generally, but I'd be lying if I said the primary motivator in this game wasn't just seeing the next fucked up little guy. This game has *a lot* of great little fucked up guys. Many epitomes of "it just keeps going". I would say my primary problem with the game is that the combat didn't quite manage to remain interesting throughout the game's runtime. By the end, I super was ready to move on. Wasn't bad, I just had a strong sense of seeing everything that there was to be seen. I do feel similarly with most simplistic turn-based RPGs. At a point, they've largely shown their hand and that so rarely is at the end of the actual game.

I do think Look Outside manages to pretty well thread the needle on humor and cosmic horror. It's easy to slip a little too into funny land and lose sight of the fact that what's happening to everyone should be spooky. I don't really know of any examples of an RPG in this style that in any way actually scared me. The top-down perspective just creates too much a sense of removal from the character. Dunno, just me. But it's certainly spooky. And as I said, legitimately quite funny. I think what I like more than either of those though, are the moments of genuine connection. The bit where you play with the tooth kid. Caring for the rat child. There's these nice little moments where we connect with another human being through all the horror and show each other care. It's sweet and I appreciate it being there.

I have no idea what all the items were for. Some, I am sure, had one use or another. But my inventory got insane and there was no obvious use for like 70% of it. The random detritus you pick up, not the consumables obviously. So no idea what's going on there. I thought it was funny, but also it kind of devalued exploration. Everywhere I went I picked up mountains of bullshit. Made going through my inventory a pain and made me feel a bit less inclined to try and find everything. Since so much of it was just...whatever? Seemingly? Unsure.

My feelings are largely positive, I definitely enjoyed my time. In particular I most enjoyed the actual setting; loved the warped apartment building and the central premise of don't go outside. Nice little inversion there. Lots of fun taking such a familiar space and warping it to total absurdity. And additionally, I really liked the slow unfolding of the building, getting access to more of it and opening up shortcuts. Good level design in that sense as well. The things that I disliked were generally pretty small and I could get over.
